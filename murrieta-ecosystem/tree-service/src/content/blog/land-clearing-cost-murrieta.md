Land clearing in Murrieta costs **$1,500–$12,000+** for a typical residential lot, depending on acreage, tree and brush density, slope, and how debris is disposed of. Most homeowners clearing a 0.25-acre lot for construction or fire compliance spend **$2,000–$5,000**. Understanding what goes into that number — before you call for quotes — helps you compare bids accurately and avoid budget surprises.

## What Types of Land Clearing Are There?

Not all clearing jobs are the same. The scope of work is the first thing a contractor will ask about, and it directly determines the price.

**Selective clearing** means removing specific trees, shrubs, or brush while keeping other vegetation intact. Homeowners use this approach to thin out an overgrown section of their property, improve sightlines, or address a problem tree without disturbing the surrounding landscape. Because it requires judgment calls on the jobsite, selective clearing is billed at a higher per-unit rate than full clearing — but the total cost is usually lower because fewer trees are being removed.

**Full lot clearing** is complete removal of all vegetation in preparation for construction, grading, or a new structure. This is the most common scenario for Murrieta homeowners building an ADU, adding a pool, or developing a vacant parcel. Equipment can cover ground quickly when there are no items to preserve, which keeps labor costs more predictable.

**CalFire compliance clearing** applies to lots in the Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone (VHFHSZ), which covers a significant portion of unincorporated Riverside County. Property owners in these zones are legally required to maintain 100 feet of defensible space or to the property line, whichever is less. The work typically involves removing dead vegetation, thinning shrubs to specific spacing requirements, and creating a lean, clean, and green zone immediately around the structure. If you've received a CalFire notice, the clearing scope is defined for you — bring that notice to your contractor.

## How Debris Disposal Affects Your Total

Debris disposal is one of the largest variables in a clearing quote, and it's easy to overlook. You have three main options:

**On-site chipping** is the most cost-effective. A chipper processes brush and smaller limbs into mulch that stays on your property — either spread across the cleared area or piled for you to haul in your truck. Most clearing quotes include basic chipping. If you can accept mulch on-site, this is the right choice.

**Haul-away to a green waste facility** adds **$500–$2,000** or more to the job depending on load volume, truck trips, and tipping fees. Riverside County green waste facilities charge by the ton. For a fully cleared 0.25-acre lot with mature vegetation, expect at least 3–6 loads. This is necessary if you're preparing for construction and need the site clean.

**Burn permits** are theoretically the cheapest disposal option, but they come with significant restrictions. Agricultural burn permits from Riverside County Fire are only valid from **November through April** and require a permit application. Burn days are declared by the South Coast Air Quality Management District (SCAQMD) — you can only burn on approved days. Burning is not permitted within Murrieta city limits. If your parcel is in unincorporated Riverside County and you have the time to wait for burn season, this can eliminate debris costs entirely. If you're on a construction timeline, it's rarely practical.

## Slope and Access: The Hidden Cost Multiplier

Flat lots with street-level equipment access are the baseline scenario all pricing tables assume. The moment those conditions change, costs rise.

**Steep hillside lots** require handheld equipment — chainsaw work, hand-carry debris to a staging area — rather than a skid steer or track loader. Labor hours increase significantly. On lots with grades over 20–25%, expect clearing costs to run **30–60% higher** than the flat-lot equivalent.

**Limited access** (narrow gates, no alley access, tight clearances) forces smaller equipment or manual methods. A 36-inch gate opening that prevents a compact track loader from entering means the crew works by hand. This adds labor hours and often a day or more to the timeline.

## Stump Grinding: Usually a Separate Line Item

Most land clearing quotes do **not** include stump grinding. After trees are felled, stumps remain in the ground. If the cleared area will be graded or built on, stumps need to be ground below grade.

Stump grinding is typically quoted separately at **$75–$400 per stump**, depending on diameter. A 12-inch diameter stump runs about $100–$150. A 36-inch stump from a large oak or eucalyptus can run $300–$400. For a lot with 15 mature trees, stump grinding alone can add $1,500–$3,000 to your total.

When requesting quotes, ask explicitly: "Does this include stump grinding to below grade?" The answer will clarify what you're comparing across contractors. ## Permits: What You Need Before Clearing Starts

**Burning:** Any open burning requires a Riverside County agricultural burn permit. Apply through the Riverside County Fire Department. Burn day authorization is separate and comes from SCAQMD — check burntoday.com on the day of your intended burn.

**Grading:** If the clearing project involves grading more than 50 cubic yards of material, a grading permit from the City of Murrieta (or Riverside County for unincorporated parcels) is required. Most land clearing projects that don't involve moving earth do not trigger this threshold — but any contractor doing significant cut-and-fill work should confirm permit status before beginning.

**CalFire inspection:** If your property is in the VHFHSZ and you've completed defensible space work, you can request a CalFire inspection to document compliance. This is optional but useful if you're selling the property or have received a compliance notice.

**City vs. county jurisdiction:** Verify whether your parcel falls within Murrieta city limits or in unincorporated Riverside County. The boundary is not always obvious. Unincorporated parcels are subject to Riverside County regulations, not city ordinances. You can check jurisdiction using the Riverside County Assessor parcel search at assessor.rivco.org.

## What Clearing Work Actually Involves

A typical residential clearing crew arrives with a chipper, a skid steer or compact track loader, and 2–4 workers. For a 0.25-acre lot with moderate brush:

- Day 1: Felling trees and larger shrubs, chipping brush
- Day 2 (if needed): Grubbing out root balls, final grading passes, debris staging

Total time for that scope is usually **1–2 days**. Larger lots, denser vegetation, or difficult terrain extend the timeline. Most reputable contractors will give you a timeline estimate with their written quote — hold them to it, especially if you have construction trades scheduled to follow.

## FAQ

### Does land clearing include stump removal?

No. Standard land clearing quotes cover felling trees and removing above-ground vegetation. Stump grinding is a separate service, typically priced at $75–$400 per stump depending on diameter. Always confirm in writing whether stump grinding is included.

### Do I need a permit to clear land in Murrieta, CA?

Usually not for basic vegetation removal. However, if the work involves open burning, you need a Riverside County agricultural burn permit. If it involves moving more than 50 cubic yards of soil, a grading permit from the city or county is required. Verify jurisdiction first — city and county rules differ.

### Can I burn cleared brush in Murrieta?

Not within Murrieta city limits. On unincorporated Riverside County parcels, burning is allowed with a permit from Riverside County Fire during the approved season (November–April) on days designated by SCAQMD. Check burntoday.com on the day you plan to burn.

### How long does land clearing take for a typical residential lot?

A 0.25-acre lot with moderate brush typically takes 1–2 days with a standard crew and equipment. Larger lots, steeper terrain, or denser vegetation extend the timeline. Confirm the estimated schedule with your contractor before signing a contract.

### What's the difference between land clearing and grading?

Land clearing removes vegetation — trees, brush, shrubs, stumps — from the surface. Grading reshapes the soil elevation itself: cutting high spots, filling low spots, and creating a level or properly sloped surface for construction. Many projects require both, done in sequence. Some contractors offer both services; others specialize in one.
