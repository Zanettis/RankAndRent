## What Is Pool Deck Resurfacing?

Resurfacing means applying a new layer of material over your existing concrete — typically ¼ to ½ inch thick — rather than tearing everything out. The process involves:

1. Pressure washing and thoroughly cleaning the deck
2. Repairing any cracks or spalled areas
3. Applying a bonding agent
4. Applying the new overlay or coating
5. Finishing (texture, color, pattern) and sealing

The result is a surface that looks brand new and performs better than the original in most cases.

## Resurfacing Methods Explained

### Spray Texture / Knockdown
The most common and affordable option. A spray gun applies a textured acrylic cement coating. The finish is rougher underfoot (good for slip resistance around water) and can be tinted. Lasts 8–12 years with proper sealing.

### Concrete Overlay
A thin cement-polymer layer that can be troweled smooth, textured, or colored. More durable than spray texture and accepts stains and dyes for custom looks. Good mid-tier option.

### Stamped Overlay
Gives the appearance of stone, tile, or brick using impression mats pressed into the overlay while wet. The most decorative option. Requires periodic resealing (every 2–3 years) because Murrieta's sun degrades sealers faster than cooler climates.

### Kool Deck / Cool Coating
A specialty coating developed specifically for pool decks that reflects heat and stays cooler underfoot on hot days. In Murrieta summers where air temps hit 105°F+, the surface temperature difference versus dark or uncoated concrete can be 30–40°F. Worth the premium for families with children.

### Exposed Aggregate Overlay
Pea gravel or colored stone is broadcast into the overlay before it sets, then the top layer is washed away to expose the aggregate. Natural-looking finish with excellent texture. Mid-to-upper pricing range.

## What Drives the Cost Up

Several factors increase the final price:

**Deck size** — Obviously larger decks cost more. Contractors may charge a higher per-sq-ft rate for smaller jobs (under 400 sq ft) because setup costs are fixed.

**Crack repair** — Minor hairline cracks are included in most resurfacing bids. Structural cracks, wide separations, or areas where the slab has shifted will cost extra to properly prepare. Expect $2–$5 per linear foot for crack prep.

**Existing surface condition** — If the deck has heavy spalling, peeling old coatings, or damaged areas, more prep work is needed before the overlay can go on. This adds labor and material cost.

**Decorative complexity** — Custom colors, multi-color patterns, or intricate stamped designs cost more than a single-color spray texture.

**Sealer and maintenance treatments** — A quality penetrating sealer is usually included in pool deck resurfacing. UV-resistant or chemical-resistant sealers cost more but last longer around pools.

**Coping and edges** — Some homeowners resurface only the flat deck and not the coping (the cap around the pool edge). If you want coping treated too, that adds square footage and sometimes a different material cost.

## When Resurfacing Makes Sense vs Full Replacement

**Resurface when:**
- The underlying concrete is structurally sound (no major cracking or shifting)
- The surface is faded, rough, or aesthetically outdated
- You want a new look without the cost and mess of demolition
- The deck is under 25 years old

**Replace when:**
- The concrete is cracked through, settling, or heaving
- Multiple areas have large spalls or missing chunks
- You want to change the deck layout (expand, reshape, add features)
- The substrate is failing (poor drainage, root damage, major soil movement)

A good rule of thumb: if the concrete is solid but ugly, resurface. If it is moving or breaking, replace.

## The Murrieta Climate Factor

Standard resurfacing products work fine in Murrieta, but UV intensity and heat cycles here are harder on pool deck finishes than in coastal areas. Two things matter:

1. **Sealer quality** — Use a UV-resistant, water-based acrylic sealer rated for outdoor use in high-heat climates. Reapply every 2 years in Murrieta (vs every 3–4 years at the coast).
2. **Color choice** — Darker colors absorb heat significantly. If bare feet are a concern, lighter tones or a Kool Deck-type coating are worth the consideration.
